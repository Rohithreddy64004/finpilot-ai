# INSURANCE EDUCATION: POLICY TERMS, WAITING PERIODS, AND CLAIMS

Plain-prose educational summaries of official IRDAI consumer and policyholder material. Coverage and claim outcomes depend on the actual contract and the facts. These notes are not a policy interpretation, legal opinion, or personalized recommendation. Source links and access dates accompany each section.

1. INSURANCE IS A CONTRACT WITH DEFINED COVER
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Health Department, Health Insurance Guidance
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI describes health insurance as a contract between a policyholder and an insurer. The insurer’s financial protection is bounded by the policy’s terms and conditions. The policy is therefore the primary place to check what events, expenses, people, limits, exclusions, and processes are covered. A product name, advertisement, or short explanation cannot establish the outcome of a particular claim. Read the policy schedule, wording, and customer information sheet, and ask the insurer for clarification where an important term is unclear. This general contract description is about health insurance material and should not be used to infer the terms of an unrelated life, motor, or other insurance product.

2. HEALTH COVER CAN TAKE DIFFERENT FORMS
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Health Insurance, Policy Holder
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI’s policyholder health-insurance material describes products that may cover hospitalization expenses, provide cashless payment at a network hospital, reimburse eligible expenses, or pay a fixed benefit when a defined illness occurs. It says the type and amount of health costs covered are specified in advance. These are different arrangements and their availability depends on the actual policy. The page explains that cashless services are connected with network hospitals and that costs outside policy limits or outside covered terms may remain payable by the policyholder. Always check the policy and insurer’s current network and claim instructions. Do not assume every health policy supports cashless treatment at every hospital.

3. WHAT A WAITING PERIOD MEANS
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Health Department, Waiting Period Guidance
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI describes a waiting period as a period specified in the policy during which specified conditions, treatments, services, or situations are not covered. The timing and the items affected depend on the policy and applicable rules. A waiting period is not a general statement that every claim is excluded during the same period. The policyholder should identify the condition or service named in the policy, the start point, and the wording that governs continuity or renewal. This summary intentionally does not give one universal duration: IRDAI materials and product terms must be checked for the current rule that applies to the exact contract. Do not infer coverage from a general definition alone.

4. PRE-EXISTING CONDITIONS AND DISCLOSURE
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Health Department, Buying Health Insurance Guidance
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI tells prospective health-insurance buyers to disclose pre-existing health problems. Its guidance warns that concealment can lead to a claim dispute and may lead to rejection of a claim or cancellation of a policy. It also says an insurer may require medical tests depending on the case and advises checking who bears the costs and following the required process. The policyholder should provide accurate information on the proposal and retain the policy documents issued after acceptance. The effect of a medical history, a waiting period, and coverage for a pre-existing condition depends on the wording and rules applicable to that policy. This explanation does not decide whether a particular condition is covered.

5. READ THE CUSTOMER INFORMATION SHEET
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Health Department, Customer Information Sheet Guidance
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI says a customer information sheet, or CIS, is provided with an insurance policy and explains basic features in simple language. The health department material lists information that a CIS should contain, including the type of insurance, sum insured, coverage, exclusions, sub-limits, deductibles, waiting periods, and certain policy servicing items. It also says the CIS includes claims procedure, policy servicing, and grievance redressal details, including contact information relevant to an Insurance Ombudsman. The CIS is a useful summary, but it does not replace the policy contract. For a disputed term, compare the CIS with the issued policy wording and ask the insurer to clarify the controlling language.

6. EXCLUSIONS, SUB-LIMITS, AND COST SHARING
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Health Department, Policy Restrictions and CIS Guidance
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI’s health guidance says buyers should look for restrictions such as room-rent or intensive-care limits, waiting periods, exclusions, sub-limits, and co-payments. It describes a co-payment as the policyholder sharing part of a claim and a sub-limit as a defined cap relevant to an expense or treatment. These terms can change how much an insurer pays even when the event relates to a covered treatment. Their effect cannot be calculated from a generic description; the applicable wording, sum insured, bills, and claim facts are needed. Before purchase, read those provisions and the customer information sheet. For a live claim, ask the insurer to identify the clause used in its decision.

7. CASHLESS TREATMENT
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/faqs-on-health-insurance-regulations
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: FAQs on Health Insurance Regulations, Cashless Facility
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI’s health-insurance FAQ describes a cashless facility as direct payment by the insurer, or a third-party administrator on its behalf, to a network provider, to the extent preauthorization is approved under the policy. The FAQ says the facility is offered at network providers that have an agreement with the insurer. IRDAI’s policyholder information also warns that expenses beyond policy limits or expenses not covered by the policy may remain the policyholder’s responsibility. Cashless is therefore a payment arrangement subject to policy terms and authorization; it is not a guarantee that every bill will be paid. Confirm network status and follow the insurer’s current preauthorization instructions.

8. REIMBURSEMENT CLAIMS
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/faqs-on-health-insurance-regulations
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: FAQs on Health Insurance Regulations, Reimbursement
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI’s FAQ says reimbursement may be available for treatment at a hospital or medical establishment subject to the terms and conditions of the policy contract and applicable licensing or registration requirements. In a reimbursement process, the policyholder pays the provider and submits a claim to the insurer for consideration under the contract. The exact documents, deadlines, and submission method must be taken from the policy and insurer’s current claims procedure; this general description is not a universal checklist. Keep the claim reference, bills, and insurer communication together as practical records, and ask the insurer to identify any missing document or policy clause if a claim is delayed or queried.

9. STARTING A HEALTH CLAIM
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Health Department, Claims Procedure and Insurer Website Guidance
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI says an insurer’s website should provide details about the policy, empanelled hospitals, the cashless claim process, reimbursement claim steps, and applicable service turnaround times. The steps can differ by product and by whether the policyholder uses a network provider. A safe starting point is to contact the insurer or the service contact identified in the policy or customer information sheet, identify whether the request is cashless or reimbursement, and follow the current instructions for authorization and documents. This is a general process description, not a claim checklist or promise of approval. A policyholder should not rely on an unofficial intermediary’s summary when the insurer’s current policy documents give different instructions.

10. POLICY DETAILS TO KEEP AVAILABLE
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Health Department, Customer Information Sheet and Claims Information
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI’s material points policyholders to the policy and CIS for coverage, exclusions, waiting periods, claim procedure, servicing, and grievance contacts. Keeping the policy schedule, wording, CIS, premium receipts, endorsements, and the insurer’s written communication accessible can help a person check the terms relevant to a question. That is practical recordkeeping guidance, not a substitute for the insurer’s required claim documents. The required forms and evidence depend on the event, policy, and insurer’s current procedure. If an answer depends on a particular clause, inspect the actual issued documents rather than inferring it from a generic product description.

11. CLAIM DECISIONS DEPEND ON POLICY TERMS AND FACTS
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/faqs-on-health-insurance-regulations
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: FAQs on Health Insurance Regulations, Claim Settlement
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI’s health FAQ states that insurance claims are to be disposed of in accordance with the policy contract and applicable regulations. A claim outcome can therefore depend on coverage wording, exclusions, preauthorization, required information, the expenses incurred, and the evidence submitted. A general guide cannot determine if a specific illness, treatment, bill, or person is covered. Neither the existence of a policy nor submission of a claim establishes that the insurer must pay the entire amount claimed. Ask the insurer to explain the decision with reference to the applicable contract terms. If the facts or medical and legal issues are complex, obtain suitable professional help.

12. WHAT TO DO WHEN A CLAIM IS DISPUTED
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/about-consumer-affairs
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Policyholders Protection and Grievance Redressal
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI advises prospects and policyholders to first take a grievance to the insurance company. Its consumer-affairs department handles grievances involving insurers and may take them up with the company for redressal. The page describes the regulator’s role as facilitating redress and, where required, carrying out investigation or enquiry. A policyholder can ask the insurer for a written explanation, the policy clause relied on, and any outstanding information required. Keep copies of the complaint and response as useful records. The regulator’s process does not itself decide that every claim must be paid; the terms, evidence, and current rules remain relevant.

13. IRDAI GRIEVANCE CHANNELS
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/grievance-redressal-mechanism1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Grievance Redressal Mechanism
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI’s grievance page says policyholders should first approach the concerned insurer’s grievance redressal cell. If the insurer does not respond within a reasonable period or the policyholder is dissatisfied, the person may approach IRDAI’s grievance redressal cell. The page lists Bima Bharosa, email, phone, and physical correspondence as channels. It also says the complainant should provide the details requested in the registration form, because incomplete information may prevent the complaint from being registered. Contact details and online processes can change, so check the current official page before using them. This summary does not reproduce personal contact details or claim that any one channel guarantees a particular outcome.

14. INSURANCE OMBUDSMAN
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/en/web/policy-holder/ombudsman
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Ombudsman, Policy Holder
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI describes the Insurance Ombudsman as a route for eligible policyholder complaints. Its page says a complainant must first have approached the insurance company and describes circumstances such as rejection, dissatisfaction, or no response. The page covers complaint categories that include claim delays, partial or total repudiation, disputes about premium, and policy-term misrepresentation. Eligibility, territorial jurisdiction, claim-value conditions, process, and time limits are governed by the current scheme. Check the official Ombudsman rules and current IRDAI page before filing; the broad summary here does not decide whether a specific complaint is eligible. Preserve the insurer’s complaint and response details when seeking further review.

15. POLICY SERVICING AND RENEWAL
Source: https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Health Department, Renewal and Policy Servicing Guidance
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI advises health policyholders to renew on time and says a delay may affect coverage of an insured event or continuity benefits. The actual renewal date, premium, grace provisions, and effect of a missed payment should be checked in the contract and current official rules. A general explanation cannot establish that a specific policy remains in force. Policyholders should keep confirmation of renewal and consult the insurer if payment or policy status is uncertain. Details of the current policy, network providers, claims process, service times, and grievance contacts should be checked on the insurer’s website and official IRDAI resources rather than inferred from another product.

Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: What We Do, Policy Holder
Publisher: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India
IRDAI’s policyholder website describes its mission as protecting policyholder interests and regulating the insurance industry. This educational file summarizes selected public IRDAI pages, but it is not an insurance contract and cannot confirm coverage, claim admissibility, or the result of a grievance. Insurance rules, insurer procedures, products, network hospitals, and contact channels may change.
