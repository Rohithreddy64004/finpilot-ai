1.  WHAT INSURANCE DOES Insurance is a contract under which an insurer
    provides specified financial protection subject to the policy’s
    terms, conditions, exclusions, limits, and payment requirements. It
    can help manage the financial effect of defined risks, but it does
    not cover every possible loss. The policy document and schedule are
    essential: a product name or sales brochure alone cannot confirm
    that a particular event, treatment, item, or person is covered. The
    insured should keep a copy of the policy, proposal details, premium
    receipts, endorsements, and communications with the insurer.

2.  COMMON INSURANCE CATEGORIES Life insurance provides benefits defined
    by the particular life policy. Health insurance may pay eligible
    medical expenses or provide benefits according to its terms. Motor
    insurance covers specified risks connected with a vehicle and may
    include legally required and optional components. Property and other
    general insurance policies address risks listed in the contract.
    These categories are not interchangeable. Two policies within the
    same broad category can have materially different conditions,
    exclusions, limits, waiting periods, and claim procedures. Users
    should compare actual policy wording, not only premiums or marketing
    summaries.

3.  PREMIUMS AND POLICY DETAILS A premium is the amount payable to
    obtain or continue coverage under the contract. Price can depend on
    factors defined by the insurer and product, but a low premium alone
    does not establish good value. Compare the coverage amount,
    deductibles or co-payments where applicable, sub-limits, exclusions,
    waiting periods, renewal conditions, claim process, service
    arrangements, and any applicable restrictions. Check the policy
    schedule and wording for the exact insured persons, dates, sum
    insured, add-ons, and endorsements. Do not assume a verbal statement
    overrides the written policy.

4.  EXCLUSIONS, WAITING PERIODS, AND LIMITS An exclusion identifies a
    situation or loss that the policy does not cover. A waiting period
    is a specified period during which certain benefits may not be
    available, depending on the contract and applicable rules. A
    deductible, co-payment, or sub-limit can affect how much the insurer
    pays. The meaning and applicability of these terms vary by policy.
    For a real claim, read the policy wording and obtain written
    clarification from the insurer if a clause is unclear. FinPilot must
    not infer coverage from a general product description or promise
    that a claim will be approved.

5.  MAKING A CLAIM Claim requirements and deadlines depend on the policy
    and type of event. Policyholders should notify the insurer or
    authorized claims administrator according to the policy
    instructions, preserve bills and supporting evidence, complete
    required forms accurately, and retain copies of submissions and
    reference numbers. For health claims, documents may include bills,
    medical records, prescriptions, discharge summaries, and other
    evidence requested under the policy. For motor or property claims,
    relevant evidence may include photographs, invoices, incident
    details, and official reports where required. These are examples,
    not a universal checklist. The insurer’s current instructions and
    policy terms control.

6.  CLAIM DENIAL OR DISAGREEMENT If a claim is delayed, reduced, or
    rejected, request the insurer’s written explanation and identify the
    policy clause or factual issue relied upon. Check whether required
    documents were supplied and whether the insurer has explained the
    next steps. Keep a timeline of calls, emails, letters, claim IDs,
    and responses. Do not assume that every rejection is unlawful or
    that every claim must be paid; the outcome depends on the contract,
    evidence, and applicable rules. For significant disputes, consider
    qualified legal or insurance advice.

7.  GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL IN INDIA IRDAI’s official consumer-affairs
    material advises policyholders and prospective policyholders to
    first approach the insurance company with their grievance. If the
    response is unsatisfactory or not received within a reasonable
    period, the person may use the official IRDAI grievance channels.
    IRDAI provides the Bima Bharosa grievance system and related
    channels; users should confirm current contact details and
    submission steps on the official IRDAI website. The Insurance
    Ombudsman is another route for eligible individual policyholder
    complaints, subject to the scheme’s current eligibility requirements
    and limits. Check the current official rules before filing and
    retain evidence of the initial complaint to the insurer.

8.  AVOIDING INSURANCE MISSELLING AND FRAUD Do not sign blank forms or
    accept a policy without reading key terms. Ensure that proposal
    information is complete and accurate; incorrect disclosures can
    affect a claim. Verify that payments go through authorized channels
    and retain receipts. Be cautious of guaranteed benefits not shown in
    the policy, pressure to buy immediately, unofficial intermediaries,
    and requests for OTPs or account passwords. If an agent’s
    explanation conflicts with the written contract, seek written
    clarification before purchase.

9.  HOW FINPILOT SHOULD ANSWER INSURANCE QUESTIONS Ask which insurance
    type and policy wording are involved when necessary. Separate
    general explanations from statements about a particular policy. Cite
    IRDAI material for grievance procedures and direct users to the
    actual insurer’s current policy documents for coverage-specific
    questions. Do not claim that an event is covered or that a claim
    will succeed without reviewing the applicable policy terms and
    facts. For urgent claims, disputes, or legal deadlines, encourage
    direct contact with the insurer and an appropriate qualified
    professional.

DOCUMENT MAINTENANCE Check IRDAI’s current policyholder pages and
applicable regulations periodically. Keep the access date with each
source. Because policy wordings and grievance processes can change,
avoid treating a copied guide as a substitute for the latest official
instructions or the individual insurance contract.
