# INDIAN INDIVIDUAL TAX SLABS — AY 2026–27

Educational summary of one Income Tax Department page. Scope is limited to ordinary slab rates for individual taxpayers below 60 years of age during the previous year, for Assessment Year 2026–27. This is not a full tax computation, legal interpretation, or personalized recommendation. The source page is live and can change; check the current official page before relying on time-sensitive information.

1. SCOPE AND READING THE TABLE
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, Tax Slabs section
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
This summary describes only the ordinary slab tables on the cited page for individual taxpayers who were less than 60 years of age at any time during the previous year, for AY 2026–27. The page also presents separate tables for older individuals and other information, but those are outside this document’s slab scope. Do not apply this table to a different assessment year, an older age category, a company or other entity, or a special-rate income category. The Department page itself says its overview is not exhaustive and directs readers to the Act, forms, rules, and notifications for complete details.

2. WHAT A SLAB RATE DESCRIBES
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, Tax Slabs section
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
The table lists an income band and a rate or a fixed amount plus a rate on the amount above a stated threshold. The words “above” and the fixed tax amount are part of the table’s calculation description. Read each row with its named regime and income range. A rate in a row should not be applied to all income from the first rupee: the table gives different treatment to successive portions of taxable income. This explanation is limited to how to read the ordinary table; it does not determine taxable income, resolve eligibility, or account for every provision that can affect a final tax liability.

3. OLD-REGIME ORDINARY SLABS
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, Tax Slabs section
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
For the stated under-60 individual category and AY 2026–27, the page shows no tax up to ₹2,50,000. It shows 5% of the amount above ₹2,50,000 for income from ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000. It shows ₹12,500 plus 20% of the amount above ₹5,00,000 for income from ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000. Above ₹10,00,000, the listed amount is ₹1,12,500 plus 30% of the amount above ₹10,00,000. These are the page’s ordinary old-regime rows for this age category. Source URL: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1

4. NEW-REGIME ORDINARY SLABS
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, Tax Slabs section
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
For the same stated age category and AY 2026–27, the new-regime table under section 115BAC shows no tax up to ₹4,00,000. It shows 5% of the amount above ₹4,00,000 from ₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000; ₹20,000 plus 10% of the amount above ₹8,00,000 from ₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000; and ₹60,000 plus 15% of the amount above ₹12,00,000 from ₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000. The remaining rows are ₹1,20,000 plus 20% above ₹16,00,000 from ₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000; ₹2,00,000 plus 25% above ₹20,00,000 from ₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000; and ₹3,00,000 plus 30% above ₹24,00,000 above ₹24,00,000. Source URL: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1

5. AN ILLUSTRATIVE SLAB-ONLY CALCULATION
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, Tax Slabs section
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
As a mathematical illustration of the rows above, suppose taxable income is ₹6,00,000 and the person is in the stated under-60 category. Using only the ordinary old-regime rows, the table yields ₹12,500 on the first two taxable bands and 20% of ₹1,00,000, or ₹20,000, in the next band: ₹32,500 before any separate rebate, surcharge, cess, or other adjustment. Using only the ordinary new-regime rows, the table yields 5% of ₹2,00,000, or ₹10,000, before any separate rebate, surcharge, cess, or other adjustment. This arithmetic is not a complete amount payable, does not establish that either regime is available or preferable for a particular person, and does not include a rebate that may apply when its conditions are met. Source URL: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1

6. DEFAULT REGIME AND THE OPTION TO CHOOSE
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, New Tax Regime Vs Old Tax Regime section
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
The page describes the new tax regime under section 115BAC as the default regime for the listed eligible taxpayer categories and says eligible taxpayers may opt out and choose the old regime. The ordinary slab rows by themselves do not decide whether a person is eligible to choose. The page distinguishes non-business cases from cases with business or professional income. It says that, in non-business cases, an option to change the default regime can be exercised each year in the return filed by the specified due date. It separately describes Form 10-IEA and timing requirements for eligible taxpayers with business or professional income. Check the current instructions and the individual’s eligibility rather than assuming one election process applies to everyone.

8. SECTION 87A REBATE IS SEPARATE FROM SLAB RATES
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, Applicable Rebate under section 87A
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
The cited page describes a rebate for resident individuals of up to 100% of income tax, subject to the listed regime-specific maximum and taxable-income condition. Its table states a maximum of ₹60,000 in the new regime where taxable income does not exceed ₹12,00,000, and a maximum of ₹12,500 in the old regime where taxable income does not exceed ₹5,00,000. These values describe a conditional rebate, not ordinary slab rates and not a universal promise that tax is zero for every person below a stated income. Eligibility, taxable-income computation, regime, and other applicable statutory conditions still matter. Keep the rebate discussion distinct from the rate table. Source URL: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1

9. SURCHARGE AND MARGINAL RELIEF ARE OUTSIDE THE SLAB TABLE
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, Applicable Surcharge Rates and Marginal Relief
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
The same page presents surcharge rates based on income limits and says that marginal relief can apply when income crosses specified thresholds. The surcharge table distinguishes old and new regimes at the highest listed income band, and the page includes a note about income under specified special-rate sections and the surcharge treatment described there. These provisions are not additional ordinary slab rows. The amount, applicable income, regime, and statutory conditions need to be checked in the official material for the relevant case. Do not compute a final liability by stopping after the slab amount when surcharge or marginal relief may be relevant. Source URL: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1

10. HEALTH AND EDUCATION CESS
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, Cess note
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
The page states that health and education cess at 4% is paid on the amount of income tax plus surcharge, if any, in both regimes. This note is separate from the ordinary rates in the slab table. It is included here as a warning against treating a slab calculation as a final tax amount. Whether and how it applies to a person’s final computation must be checked with the current rules and the complete applicable facts. Source URL: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1

11. DEDUCTIONS AND EXEMPTIONS AFFECT COMPARISON
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
Accessed: 2026-10-17
Source title: Salaried Individuals for AY 2026-27, New Tax Regime Vs Old Tax Regime section
Publisher: Income Tax Department, Government of India
The Department describes the old regime as allowing various deductions and exemptions and the default new regime as having lower rates with fewer deductions, subject to specified exceptions. This summary does not list every deduction, exception, or eligibility condition, and a reader should not infer that a particular deduction is allowed in both regimes. The correct comparison depends on the person’s admissible taxable income, the regime the person can validly use, eligible deductions and exemptions, and other rules that apply to the year. The ordinary rates alone cannot establish which regime is better. Source URL: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
